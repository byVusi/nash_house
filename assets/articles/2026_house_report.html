Nash House in Review

Christopher Chapman, Head of House


If there is one thing this year taught us, it is that the true strength of a House is not measured by the trophies or wins, but by the way its boys stand together through every victory and every setback. Although not every Inter-House event went as planned, through both the wins and the losses, there were always laughs, smiles and an undeniable sense of Nash gees.

A core memory of mine from this year was a Nash braai we had on Mitchel. Although eating a boerie roll with a friend, laughing about nothing in particular is always fun, a highlight for me was our intense game of baseball cricket with Mr Mathonsi. You would have thought he was our head cricket coach after watching him smash a tennis ball over 5 boys heads, while slowly finishing his home run.

Although not every Inter-House event went as planned, through both the wins and the losses, there were always laughs, smiles and an undeniable sense of Nash gees.

A perfect example of this came before Inter-House Swimming, when our two instrumentalists, Lehoekwe and Ntsako, took to the trumpet and saxophone, leading us in war cries written by the Nash prefects. This remains one of my favourite memories of the year, every member of the Nash House brotherhood singing and chanting together in our common room. The noise was so infectious that it even drew the attention of Luke Shipway, who couldn’t hide his envy of the Nash gees. The moment he tried to step into our common room, the boys of Nash House made sure he didn’t overstay his welcome, chasing him all the way back to Hodgson.

To start off the year, Nash headed to the pool like a wave crashing onto the beach, excited to begin what promised to be a morning filled with competition and swimming. Although Nash didn’t take home the win, there was one moment that really stuck with me. As someone who is extremely competitive, I felt the pressure of doing well sitting on my shoulders. But as I stepped onto the diving block and looked up to see a wall of blue, shoulder to shoulder, screaming a Nash remix war cry, my nerves were immediately put at ease. In that moment, I realised that being a part of this incredible House was about far more than having a place to sleep and work, it was about being part of a brotherhood.

Although Nash House finished seventh, the year had only just begun. More importantly, I could already see how invested the boys, from Remove all the way through to UV, were in the House and how much each and every one of them wanted to get involved.

As our first Instagram post of the year read, “A MARATHON IS NEVER WON IN ITS FIRST 100 METRES.” We bounced back quickly, with both our seniors and juniors winning Inter-House Basketball, proving that our setback at Inter-House Swimming was simply the setup for our comeback on the basketball court.

As the winter season came to a close, Inter-House Hockey began, with the Nash juniors narrowly losing to Hill in the final. The following day, I arrived to watch the seniors with a bruised face and a few stitches on the inside of my lip from an injury. However, our senior hockey goalkeeper unexpectedly had to go home, leaving me as the only option.

Having not touched a hockey stick since Grade 7, and having never played goalkeeper before, I would be lying if I said I wasn’t scared. But as I put on the mask and my teammates told me to just have fun, I realised I was in safe hands. We ended up finishing fourth after I let in a few questionable goals, but, more importantly, we had an incredible time as a team, and that is what Inter-House sport is really about.

Across every event that took place this year, whether we won or lost, the Nashtonians showed up and gave it their all. Whether it was on stage, in the pool, on the sports field, or even standing on the sidelines supporting a fellow Nash boy, one thing became incredibly clear: a family had been built.

It is a family that I believe will continue to support one another long after our College days are over, and for that I will forever be grateful.

For many years, Nash House has lived by the words: “A DREAM FOR MANY, AND A REALITY FOR FEW.” Looking back on my five years in this House, those words mean more to me now than ever before. The trophies, results and Inter-House standings will eventually be forgotten, but the memories, friendships and brotherhood will stay with me forever.

I will always be grateful that I was one of the few lucky enough to call Nash House home.
